Focus area

Construction & Infrastructure

Independent engineering for the built environment — from the first site appraisal through construction, commissioning, defects and decades of operation.

Overview

The technical position, established properly

Construction problems are usually decided by facts that nobody gathered early enough: the condition of the ground, the capacity of an installation, the real sequence of what happened on site. We collect those facts while they are still recoverable.

We act for clients, lenders, investors and insurers — and occasionally jointly with a legal adviser on the same project. Because the practice sits alongside our lawyers, a claim can be assessed technically and documented in one pass rather than after a round of correspondence.

Services in detail

What we do, and what you get

Site appraisal and feasibility

Ground and constraint review, access and services assessment, buildability and programme realism, whole-life cost comparison between options, and a written recommendation. We are explicit about what would have to be true for a scheme to succeed.

Design review and specification audit

Independent review of design intent, coordination between disciplines, specification adequacy, code and standards compliance, and constructability — before the design is frozen, while changes are still cheap.

Technical due diligence

Condition, capacity, remaining useful life, maintenance history and compliance review of plant, buildings and infrastructure, reported against the specific risks a purchaser, lender or investor is actually carrying.

Project and construction management

Client-side management and monitoring of progress, quality, cost and safety. We verify that the work matches the specification, report against the programme, and flag drift early enough for it to be corrected.

Claims analysis and expert evidence

Delay and disruption analysis, variation valuation, defect assessment and expert reports for negotiation, adjudication or proceedings. Facts, analysis and opinion are kept clearly separate so the report survives cross-examination.

Commissioning, defects and handover

Witnessing and reviewing commissioning and testing, checking that performance guarantees are actually met, tracking the defects period, and confirming the handover pack matches what was built.

Questions

Construction & Infrastructure FAQs

Who do you act for on a construction project?

Clients, developers, lenders, investors, insurers and occasionally public bodies. We do not act for contractors on the same project as a client instruction, because the value of independent judgement disappears the moment we are on both sides.

Can you act as expert witness?

Yes. We prepare expert reports for negotiation, adjudication and court proceedings within our fields, and we will give you an honest view early on whether the evidence supports a case or whether it should be settled.

How quickly can you attend site?

For an urgent condition or programme issue we can usually attend within a few days. Site visits are quoted as a fixed fee with a written record of what was inspected and what was not.

Do you work alongside our lawyers?

Yes, and it is usually the better arrangement. Our legal and engineering practices sit in the same firm, so a contract and a condition assessment can be produced together. We also work comfortably with external counsel where instructed.

What does technical due diligence actually cover?

It depends on the asset and the risk you are taking, but typically: physical condition, installed and demonstrated capacity, remaining useful life, maintenance and defect history, outstanding statutory compliance, and the capital expenditure likely to be required. We scope it against your transaction, not a standard template.
